Summary

WhenWasI records the places you go so you can look them up later. That history is yours. It is stored on your iPhone, and — if you are signed in to iCloud — backed up to your own private iCloud account.

The developer of WhenWasI cannot see any of it. There is no account to create, no server operated by the developer, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party SDKs.

The app contains no networking code other than Apple's CloudKit, which communicates only with your own private iCloud database.

What the app stores on your device

Everything below lives in a database inside the app's own container on your iPhone. iOS deletes that container when you delete the app.

What leaves your device

Only visits and places, and only into your own private iCloud database (CloudKit container iCloud.com.stevesien.WhenWasI), and only when you are signed in to iCloud on the device.

Location fixes and diagnostic events never leave your device. They are deliberately excluded from sync.

That iCloud data sits in your personal iCloud account and is governed by Apple's privacy policy. The developer has no access to it and no ability to read, export, or recover it.

If you are not signed in to iCloud, or you turn iCloud off for this app, the app records and works exactly as before and nothing is uploaded.

Nothing is sent anywhere else. There is no other server.

How that iCloud data is protected

Apple encrypts private CloudKit data in transit and at rest. To be precise about what that does and does not mean: WhenWasI stores its fields as ordinary CloudKit values rather than end-to-end encrypted ones, so under standard iCloud settings Apple holds the keys and could in principle access the data — for example in response to a valid legal request. If you turn on Advanced Data Protection in iOS, your CloudKit data becomes end-to-end encrypted and Apple can no longer read it.

In either case the developer has no access whatsoever.

Permissions the app asks for

Location — "Always." The app uses iOS visit monitoring in the background to notice when you arrive somewhere and when you leave. It requests approximately 100-metre accuracy, not precise continuous tracking, and it does not record a continuous trail of your movement. Without "Always" access, recording stops; the app tells you so on the timeline. You can change or revoke this at any time in Settings.

Photos. Used when you open the Assistant or search, and when you ask the app to check your library against your places. The app reads the creation date and the embedded location (geotag) of photos in order to show what you took on a given day, to let you add a geotagged photo's location to your timeline, and to count how many geotagged photos sit within 100, 300, and 500 metres of each of your places. Only those dates and locations are read, and the counting happens on the device. Photos are read only — the app never modifies, uploads, or transmits your photos, and thumbnails are displayed on-device only.

Calendar. Used only when you open the Assistant or search. The app reads events for the day or search term you ask about, to help you remember where you were. Calendar data is displayed on-device only. It is never stored in the app's database and never uploaded.

Mail. The app does not read your mail and does not ask for permission to. The "Search Mail" button copies your search text to the clipboard and opens Apple's Mail app so you can paste it into Mail's own search field. That is the whole of the interaction.

Your control over the data

  • Delete a single stay — swipe it away on the timeline.
  • Delete a place — deletes that place and every visit recorded there.
  • Erase all history — deletes every visit, place, and location fix on the device, and deletes the entire record zone from your iCloud account. This cannot be undone.
  • Delete the app — iOS destroys the app's local container. Data previously synced to your iCloud account remains there until you erase it from within the app, or remove it via iOS Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage.

Recording continues after an erase unless you also turn off location access in Settings.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects nothing that would identify anyone. No data is transmitted to the developer at any age.

App Store privacy label

The app's App Store privacy label is Data Not Collected, because no data is transmitted to the developer or to any third party.
